## Rules

### Every control comes in three tiers, and the primitive draws the field frame

A control family is named after its prefab: `TextInput` → `TextField` → `TSFTextField`. Pick the lowest tier that does the job.

1. **The primitive**, such as `TextInput` or `Select`, owns the control and its field frame. Its root takes the field's states, draws the frame, owns the ids, the requiredness and the blur, and hands its state to every part; the label, description and error are parts the caller places inside it. Take it whenever the anatomy differs from an ordinary field: a control in a toolbar, two controls sharing one group, a label beside rather than above. Its `Label` part still names the control, and its `Description` and `Error` still describe it.
2. **The prefab**, such as `TextField`, assembles the primitive's parts with `Field` into the ordinary case: a label above a full-width control, then the description, then the error. It is built from the parts, never a second implementation of them, and it takes no prop that switches it to another anatomy. A control that should not be a stacked field drops to the primitive.
3. **The connector**, such as `TSFTextField`, connects the prefab to TanStack Form. It is registered in the form hook an app builds with `createAppForm` and reached as `field.TextField` inside `form.AppField`, so a call site never types its prefix. [Setting up forms](?path=/docs/design-system-setting-up-forms--docs#one-form-hook-per-app) shows how an app builds it.

### Each tier takes the field props nothing else supplies

The prefab takes them all at the top level: `label`, `description`, `errors`, `requiredness`, `dirty`, `touched` and `pending`. The connector takes `label`, `description` and `requiredness`, with the same meaning; the form supplies `errors`, `dirty`, `touched` and `pending`, so the connector takes none of them. The primitive's root takes the states, `requiredness` included; the label, the description and the requiredness suffix are parts placed inside it.

```tsx
<form.AppField name="email">
	{field => (
		<field.EmailField
			label="Email"
			description="We send the receipt here."
			requiredness="required"
		/>
	)}
</form.AppField>
```

### Requiredness is stated, never inferred

`requiredness` is `"required"`, `"optional"`, or left out, and left out shows neither mark. Nothing reads it from a schema and there is no default. A field in a form states one or the other; only a field outside a form, which asks nothing of anyone, leaves it out. A required label carries the mark, which is decoration: the control's own `required` tells assistive tech. An optional label trails the `CopyProvider`'s `optional`, which is part of the control's name.

### A label renders its children, and the suffix is a part

The prefab adds the suffix for you. On the primitive, a label renders only its children, so the suffix is `Field.Requiredness`, placed inside the label after its text. It reads `requiredness` from the root it sits in, or from its own prop where no root carries it.

```tsx
<TextInput.Root
	value={value}
	onValueChange={setValue}
	requiredness="optional">
	<TextInput.Label>
		Notes <Field.Requiredness />
	</TextInput.Label>
	…
</TextInput.Root>
```

A label beside its control, such as a lone checkbox's, keeps the required mark with its text and puts the optional word on a row of its own: `Field.RequiredMark` goes inside `Field.InlineLabelText`, and `Field.InlineOptional` follows it.

```tsx
<Checkbox.Root requiredness="optional">
	<Checkbox.Label>
		<Checkbox.Control>…</Checkbox.Control>
		<Field.InlineLabelText>
			Send me a copy <Field.RequiredMark />
		</Field.InlineLabelText>
		<Field.InlineOptional />
	</Checkbox.Label>
</Checkbox.Root>
```

### The error is the only live region for the field's validity, after the description

`Field.Error` takes errors in any shape `normalizeFieldErrors` accepts: strings, and anything with a string `message`, such as a Standard Schema issue. It renders `ErrorText` as an alert, after `Field.Description`, and both chain into the control's `aria-describedby`. A control never announces the same message a second time: what else it announces, such as a copy's outcome or an options list's load state, is never an error. Any line of error makes the field invalid.

### A wait inside a field is a spinner inlay, and the field says it is busy

A field waiting on background work, such as an async validator, mounts `Field.Pending` once the wait is worth showing, and projects `pending` through `fieldStateAttributes`, which sets `aria-busy`. `Field.Pending` is the inlay; the spinner is its child, and is hidden from assistive tech. A primitive's `Pending` mounts itself once its root's wait is worth showing.

```tsx
<TextInput.Pending>
	<Spinner size={1} />
</TextInput.Pending>
```

### What sits beside a label shares its row

A link or a counter that belongs beside a label, such as "Forgot password?" or `TextInput.Counter`, goes in `Field.LabelRow` with the label, in place of the bare label. The row keeps the label at the start and the rest at the end, and wraps on a narrow field. Every primitive takes it, and in a field group it takes the label's row. It reads `--kit-field-label-row-gap` and `--kit-field-label-row-align`.

```tsx
<TextInput.Root
	value={password}
	onValueChange={setPassword}>
	<Field.LabelRow>
		<TextInput.Label>Password</TextInput.Label>
		<Link href="/reset">Forgot password?</Link>
	</Field.LabelRow>
	<TextInput.Group>
		<TextInput.Input type="password" />
	</TextInput.Group>
</TextInput.Root>
```

### Every control reads its states through `useFieldState`

`useFieldState` reads each state from its owner: invalid from the errors, disabled from the control's own prop or a busy `Form.Root`, and pending through `useBusyPhase`, so a wait shows only once it has lasted. A control projects what it returns with `fieldStateAttributes` and never assembles the states by hand. Validity is one state, `"valid"` or `"invalid"`, so a field can never claim both.
