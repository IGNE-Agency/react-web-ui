# States

A control on a screen has no weight, no click and no give under your finger. All it can do to show it heard you is change how it looks. States are those small changes, and they are how a flat screen keeps up its half of the conversation.

<Illustration caption="Up close, the keyboard is on one control at a time, and the focus ring says which.">
	<StatesDrawing />
</Illustration>

## A control that answers back

Every state answers a question the user is silently asking. Hover says "yes, you can use this". Press says "got it". Focus says "you are here", to someone moving with the keyboard. Selected says "this is the one you picked", and disabled says "not now".

The answers have to be quick, quiet and the same everywhere. Quick, because a control that responds late feels broken. Quiet, because a state is a passing remark, not a new design: the control is still recognisably itself. And the same everywhere, because a user learns what hover looks like once, on one control, and expects it on all the rest.

</Illustration>

## The state layer

The easiest way to keep a state quiet is to leave the control's colours alone and lay something thin over them. A translucent wash of the control's own text colour, a little stronger the more is happening, reads as the same control, just more awake. Because it's made from the colour already there, it works on any fill, in any theme, without anyone choosing a hover colour.

That's how the kit draws hover and press. The layer gets a little stronger with each state: 8% for hover, 10% for focus, 12% for press and 16% for drag. Every state works a little harder in the dark theme, where a faint tint over a dark fill is easier to miss.

## The focus ring

For someone who moves with the keyboard, focus is the cursor. Lose sight of it and they're lost on the page, with no way of knowing what the next key press will do. So focus has to be visible on everything, against every background, and it has to look the same wherever it lands, so it's recognised at a glance.

The kit draws one ring for all of it: in the brand colour, three pixels wide, two pixels outside the element's edge, following the element's own corners. It never changes shape or colour from one control to the next.
