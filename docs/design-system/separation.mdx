# Separation

A screen is a handful of regions, and nobody can use any of them until they can see where one ends and the next begins. There are only a few ways to tell two things apart, and they are not equally quiet: the quieter the one that does the job, the calmer the screen.

<Illustration caption="Every box adds noise. A page boxed in by hairlines feels cramped, while the one tile with room around it needs no line at all.">
	<SeparationDrawing />
</Illustration>

## Telling things apart

Distance is the first thing the eye reads. Two things close together look like one group, and a gap between them says they're two, without adding a single mark. A change of ground comes next: a region on a slightly different surface reads as its own place, the way a sheet of paper stands out on a desk. Height says something floats above the rest, for a moment. A line is the most explicit of the four, and the noisiest, because every line is one more thing to look at.

So each step up the list costs a little more of the reader's attention. Reach for the quietest one that works, and stop there.

There are two familiar ways to get this wrong. The first wraps every panel, toolbar and strip in a hairline until the page feels boxed in and cramped. The second gives everything a shadow, until the page is a stack of floating cards that all claim to be on top, and none of them stands out.

## Quietest first

The kit works down the list in order: space, then a step along [the surface tiers](?path=/docs/design-system-foundation-colour--docs#surfaces), then elevation, and a line only when none of those will do. Elevation is only ever for something that genuinely floats, and only when its surface alone didn't carry it: [anything in the page's flow carries no shadow](?path=/docs/design-system-foundation-elevation--docs#an-element-that-is-part-of-the-page-carries-no-shadow).

## When a line earns its place

A line belongs where the line is the thing itself, not a fence around something. Without its outline, a text field reads as static text, and an unchecked box has no fill to tell it from the surface behind it. A separator between table rows or menu groups exists to be a line. So does the underline that marks the selected tab, and a variant whose whole point is to be outlined. Everywhere else, space, a surface or height does the job.

<Details title="The four cases that justify a line">

A line is drawn only for these. Everything else uses space, surface or elevation. Explained in [When a line earns its place](#when-a-line-earns-its-place).

1. **A text-entry control or checkbox.** Without an outline an input reads as static text, and an unchecked box has no fill to distinguish it from the surface behind it.
2. **A separator whose entire job is to be a line**, between table rows or menu groups.
3. **An active indicator**, such as the underline on a selected tab.
4. **A variant that exists to be outlined.**
