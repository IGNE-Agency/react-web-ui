# Patterns

A product is built from components, but people meet it in moments, and in families. The moments are waiting for something to load, finding nothing, watching something fail, being told that something happened and being asked whether they are sure. The families are components that share a job: the fields you type in, the labels pinned to things, the navigation around every page and the surfaces that float above it. A pattern answers one moment or one family once, so that every component it touches behaves the same way.

<Illustration caption="Mixed parts fall into families by what they share.">
	<PatternsDrawing />
</Illustration>

## Applies to

- **Regions of data:** [data tables](?path=/docs/components-data-table--docs), [comment threads](?path=/docs/components-conversation--docs), [stale content](?path=/docs/components-stale--docs), the menus of [selects](?path=/docs/components-select-field--docs) and [comboboxes](?path=/docs/components-combobox-field--docs), and [popovers](?path=/docs/components-popover--docs) that load details.
- **Telling people what happened:** [toasts](?path=/docs/components-toast--docs), banners, and the [status messages](?path=/docs/components-status-message--docs) for [loading](?path=/docs/components-loading-message--docs), [nothing found](?path=/docs/components-empty-message--docs) and [failure](?path=/docs/components-error-message--docs).
- **Fields and forms:** [forms](?path=/docs/components-form--docs), [text fields](?path=/docs/components-text-field--docs), [password fields](?path=/docs/components-password-field--docs), [text areas](?path=/docs/components-textarea-field--docs), [number fields](?path=/docs/components-number-field--docs), [selects](?path=/docs/components-select-field--docs), [comboboxes](?path=/docs/components-combobox-field--docs), [date fields](?path=/docs/components-date-field--docs), [date range fields](?path=/docs/components-date-range-field--docs), [time fields](?path=/docs/components-time-field--docs), [checkboxes](?path=/docs/components-checkbox-field--docs), [checkbox groups](?path=/docs/components-checkbox-group-field--docs), [radio groups](?path=/docs/components-radio-group-field--docs), [segmented controls](?path=/docs/components-segmented-control-field--docs), [toggle groups](?path=/docs/components-toggle-group-field--docs), [switches](?path=/docs/components-switch-field--docs), [file fields](?path=/docs/components-file-field--docs), [photo fields](?path=/docs/components-photo-field--docs), [one-time code fields](?path=/docs/components-otp-field--docs) and the preset fields for [email](?path=/docs/components-email-field--docs), [phone numbers](?path=/docs/components-phone-field--docs) and [search](?path=/docs/components-search-field--docs).
- **Labels pinned to things:** [tags](?path=/docs/components-tag--docs), [tag groups](?path=/docs/components-tag-group--docs), [badges](?path=/docs/components-badge--docs) and the [filter bar](?path=/docs/components-filter-bar--docs).
- **Navigation:** [the nav](?path=/docs/components-nav--docs), as a rail or a bar, [tabs](?path=/docs/components-tabs--docs) and [breadcrumbs](?path=/docs/components-breadcrumbs--docs).
- **Above the page:** [tooltips](?path=/docs/components-tooltip--docs), [menus](?path=/docs/components-menu--docs), [popovers](?path=/docs/components-popover--docs), [dialogs](?path=/docs/components-dialog--docs), the confirmation dialog, [drawers](?path=/docs/components-drawer--docs) and [toasts](?path=/docs/components-toast--docs).

## The life of a region

Data arrives over a network, so every part of a screen that shows it lives through the same few moments. First it waits. Then it shows what it found, or finds nothing, or fails. A screen handles those moments well when each one happens in place: in the region it concerns, without taking down anything around it, and without making the reader look for it somewhere else.

<Illustration caption="A region fails in its own place, and everything around it stands.">
	<RegionDrawing />
</Illustration>

Waiting comes first. It shows in the region that waits, [late enough not to flicker, and then for long enough not to flash](?path=/docs/design-system-foundation-motion--docs#a-wait-announces-itself-late-and-then-stays-a-moment), and [in the shape of what is coming](?path=/docs/design-system-foundation-motion--docs#a-wait-whose-shape-is-known-ahead-of-its-content-is-drawn-as-that-shape) when that shape is known. A whole page waits behind a loading message; [a button waits behind a spinner](?path=/docs/components-button--docs#a-button-whose-handler-awaits-carries-a-moving-indicator) in its own place.

Finding nothing is an answer too, and it belongs where the answer would have been. The message takes the place of the rows, under the same headers and filters, so the reader can change what they asked for. It says in one sentence that nothing was found, and offers nothing it can't follow through.

## Failing gracefully

A failure is the moment a screen is most tempted to throw everything away, and the moment the reader can least afford it. When a region already shows data and a refresh fails, the data stays and the failure is reported above it. Only a region with nothing to show puts the failure in place of its content.

## Telling people what happened

News is easiest to take in where the eye already is. Feedback about what is in front of the reader appears right there. A toast is for something that happened out of view, usually after they moved on. A dialog interrupts everything, so it is kept for a question that has to be answered before anything else.

<Illustration caption="The row that changed says so at its own end.">
	<ToldDrawing />
</Illustration>

| What happened | Said where | For example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Data in view didn't load | In the region | "Comments could not be loaded." |
| A field is invalid | Under the field | "A KvK number has 8 digits." |
| A form did its job | In place of its introduction | "A recovery link is on its way." |
| Background work failed | A toast, with the fix | A comment that didn't send |
| A condition that lasts | A banner across the app | "Storage almost full" |
| The session ended | A toast at sign-in | "You were signed out" |
| A step can't be undone | A dialog | "Delete comment?" |

A toast that offers an action waits until it is dismissed, because a fix that disappears before it can be pressed is no fix. Any other toast leaves after a few seconds, and the same toast is never shown twice at once.

## Where you type

A form field is a promise that you can type here, and its outline is what makes the promise: without it, an entry reads as static text, which is why a field is one of [the four cases that justify a line](?path=/docs/design-system-principles-separation--docs#the-four-cases-that-justify-a-line). People learn what a field looks like from the first form they fill in and expect every field after it to look the same, so a field that draws its edge, its focus or its error its own way reads as a different kind of thing.

Here every text-entry control takes one shared shell: a card-surface fill, a hairline border that warms to the brand colour on hover and focus, a small radius, the same padding, [the focus ring](?path=/docs/design-system-principles-interaction--docs#everything-you-can-reach-with-the-keyboard-shows-the-focus-ring), and the disabled and invalid treatments. No field writes its own.

</Illustration>

The browser draws its calendar, so it takes none of our surface, radius or highlight. It differs from browser to browser, down to which segments exist. And it stays stubbornly light under a dark theme. The date, date range and time fields are the primitive library's date parts inside our own frame, which is what buys the styling back.

Invalid is the whole field turning: error ink on an error fill, inside an error border. A red outline around a field that otherwise looks untouched reads as a highlight, not as a problem.

Behind those signals sits one vocabulary of states. Every control publishes its states the same way, and every stylesheet reads them from there, never from the browser's own states or a library's. The two primitive libraries publish different sets: one knows filled, dirty and touched but never read-only, and the other knows read-only and none of those three. Neither covers the other, so a rule written against either one's names quietly does nothing for half the fields, and the field that lost its treatment looks correct until someone opens it in the missing state.

No single place holds those states. Each is read from whatever owns it: the field's own settings for disabled, read-only and required, the validator for validity, the form for dirty and touched, the value for filled, the primitive for focus, and the shared busy timing for pending.

Read-only is a dashed border on the raised surface, not a slightly different fill. It is the app's one read-only signal, so every control that can be read-only wears it: a text field, a select, a combobox, a segmented group, and the box of a checkbox, radio or switch. It is the control's own border in its own place, never a second edge drawn beside it.

## Everything in one box

Plenty of fields hold more than the text you type: a currency sign before an amount, a unit after it, a button that clears it, a calendar to pick a date from. To the reader it is still one field, one box with one border, and adding a part shouldn't change its shape. Each part has to fit inside without bringing a second edge, a second height or a second target.

Here a field that shares its box with anything else, like a leading glyph, a unit, a clear button, a spinner or a calendar trigger, is a field group. The group, not the control, carries the border, the radius, the fill and the padding, and the focus ring whenever focus is anywhere inside it. Because the group isn't the control, it can't see the browser's own disabled or focus states, so it takes every treatment from the one vocabulary of states.

Anything inside a group is bare. The control drops its border, background and padding, and takes its colour and type from the group. A control that keeps its own shell inside a group is two boxes and two focus rings, which is easy to miss the first time a part is added to a field that started out on its own.

## Labels pinned to things

A chip, a badge or a tag is a word pinned to something else: a status on a row, a count on a tab, a filter that's switched on. It annotates, so it has to stay quieter than the thing it belongs to. And its colour carries meaning, so the same tone has to mean the same thing on every screen.

Here anything that carries a tone and a variant colours itself from one shared table of tones, which sets its fill, its ink and its border. Sharing one table is what stops these drifting: a new tone lands everywhere at once. The solid variant swaps [the role's own pair](?path=/docs/design-system-foundation-token-architecture--docs#a-component-reads-a-role-never-a-step) rather than bringing in a fourth colour, so it follows the theme.

Shape and size come from one shared tag shell, which wraps the table of tones and adds the pill. A chip sits at the small label size. It is usually one of several in a row or a cell, and at body size a row of them is the loudest thing on the page instead of a note on the thing it belongs to.

## Finding your way

Navigation is the part of a product people stop seeing once they know it, and that is the point. It has to say where you are and where else you can go, and otherwise stay out of the way of the page it frames. The surest way to stay out of the way is to stay the same: the same place and the same marks, on every page.

<Illustration caption="Navigation keeps to its edge, says where you are, and leaves the rest of the screen to the page.">
	<RailDrawing />
</Illustration>

Here a navigation item is a full-height pill. The selected one is a tonal fill with the stronger ink of the label role, and [hover and press come from the shared state layer](?path=/docs/design-system-principles-interaction--docs#hover-and-press-come-from-the-shared-state-layer). <Pull>Rails and bars are told apart from the canvas by their surface, never by a border or a shadow.</Pull> That is [how any region in the page's flow is set apart](?path=/docs/design-system-principles-separation--docs#a-region-in-the-pages-flow-is-told-apart-by-its-surface-tier-not-a-hairline), and a bottom bar is no exception: it sits in the page's flow, so [it isn't floating and carries no shadow](?path=/docs/design-system-foundation-elevation--docs#an-element-that-is-part-of-the-page-carries-no-shadow).

The top bar of the app's frame owns the page title and the tab strip, and both come from the tree of routes. A page never draws its own top-level heading or tab strip, and never pushes a header up into the frame, which would send state the wrong way through the tree.

## Above the page

Some surfaces float over the page for a moment: a tooltip that names a button, a menu, a list of options, a dialog that asks a question, a drawer of filters. They share a job, to say something now and then get out of the way. So they should look like one family, letting a reader tell at a glance what is floating and how to send it away.

Here tooltips, menus, select lists and popovers sit at [the first floating level](?path=/docs/design-system-foundation-elevation--docs#levels) on the overlay surface, a tooltip at the tightest radius and a list at a card's. Toasts sit one level up, on a tone container. A dialog takes the largest radius. None of them is outlined.

Menus, select lists and typeahead lists are the same object behind different triggers, so they share one surface and one row. The row brings its highlight and [the deliberate absence of a focus ring](?path=/docs/design-system-principles-interaction--docs#a-list-whose-highlight-follows-mouse-and-keyboard-takes-no-ring-on-top), which would mark a row twice that the highlight already marks.

A dialog or a drawer [carries no shadow](?path=/docs/design-system-foundation-elevation--docs#a-shadow-is-the-last-resort-not-the-first). <Pull>Its darkened, blurred backdrop already says it sits over the page, so a shadow on top says nothing a reader can use.</Pull> That backdrop stays restrained, enough to push the page back without hiding what the user opened the surface to act on: a one-pixel blur, and a lighter scrim under a drawer.

Two minutes before it ends, a dialog asks whether the reader is still there, counts down, and offers to stay signed in or to sign out now. It can only be [closed by answering it](#a-dismissable-surface-says-so-on-its-titles-line). Any request the app makes in the meantime resets the clock and closes the dialog. When the session does end, the sign-in page says so in a toast that stays until it is dismissed.

<Details title="A field's states are read from one projected vocabulary, never from native or library attributes">

`fieldStateAttributes` projects every state onto the markup as `data-*` attributes, and every stylesheet keys off those rather than off `:disabled`, `[aria-invalid]` or a library's own attribute.
