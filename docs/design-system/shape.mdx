# Shape

Shape is where a thing's edges meet. It says more than you'd expect: square corners feel exact and technical, round ones feel soft and friendly, and one set of corners used everywhere is a big part of what makes a product feel made by one hand.

<Illustration caption="Where two edges meet, the corner sets the character.">
	<ShapeDrawing />
</Illustration>

## The character of a corner

The rounder a corner, the softer the thing feels. A sharp corner reads as precise and dense, a generous one as calm and approachable, and a shape rounded all the way into a pill or a circle reads as something you can press.

Radius also has to grow with size. The same small curve that looks just right on a tag looks pinched on a dialog, and a curve big enough for the dialog swallows the tag. So a system doesn't pick one radius: it picks a short scale, from small for dense details to large for big surfaces, and every element takes the step that suits its size.

<Illustration caption="One shape, rounded a little more each time: from exact and technical on the left to a pill and a circle on the right.">
	<SoftnessDrawing />
</Illustration>

## Corners within corners

When one rounded thing sits inside another, like a card on a sheet, the two curves have to agree. Give them the same radius and the gap between them swells at every corner. Draw both curves around one shared centre, so the inner radius is the outer one minus the gap, and the space between them stays even all the way round the bend.

<Illustration caption="One corner of a card holding another, drawn huge. Both curves share a centre, so the gap between them stays the same width round the bend.">
	<ConcentricDrawing />
</Illustration>

That's for surfaces. A control keeps its own shape wherever it sits: [a button is a pill or a circle](?path=/docs/components-button--docs#a-button-is-a-pill-or-a-circle-wherever-it-sits), never a radius matched to the container around it.

## The radii

The kit has a small set of named radii, from dense chrome, like menu rows and tags inside a table cell, through inputs, cards and dialogs to bottom sheets, plus a pill and a circle.

<Specimen caption="Each radius drawn as the corner it rounds, at three times its true size: the dashed lines are the square corner it replaces, the dashed circle the one that rounds it.">
	<RadiusSpecimen />
</Specimen>

Radius is a shape decision and never a spacing one. Rounding a corner by a spacing value ties it to [the spacing scale](?path=/docs/design-system-foundation-spacing--docs#the-scale), and every corner comes out a hard-looking couple of pixels.

## No sharp edges

A square corner anywhere is a defect, not a neutral default — on anything painted, not just the obvious cards: a hover or selection fill, a focus ring, a skeleton, a scroll thumb, a drag handle, a divider that is really a bar, a full-bleed row inside a rounded container.

The scale runs from the smallest radius to the largest precisely so that dense chrome has somewhere to go. <Pull>Nothing is too small to round: a fill four pixels tall takes the smallest radius.</Pull> No radius at all is for the one case that is not a corner — an edge that continues past the element, like a header band spanning a card whose own corners are already rounded, or a stacked group's inner joins.

The failure this catches is a shape that is only visible in one state, because nobody sees it in the file. [A hover fill from the shared state layer](?path=/docs/design-system-principles-interaction--docs#hover-and-press-come-from-the-shared-state-layer) drawn on a control with no radius of its own is a hard-edged rectangle that appears under the pointer and vanishes, and it is the most common place the aesthetic breaks.

## Technical rules

Every rule on this page, one sentence each, for looking up rather than reading. Open one for the details and the code.

<Details title="A corner's radius grows with the size of what it rounds">

Dense details take the small radii and big surfaces the large ones, so a tag and a dialog each look rounded in proportion. Explained in [The character of a corner](#the-character-of-a-corner).

</Details>

<Details title="A corner inside a corner shares its centre">

A rounded surface nested in another takes the outer radius minus the gap between them, so the gap stays even round the bend. Explained in [Corners within corners](#corners-within-corners).

</Details>

<Details title="A control keeps its own shape wherever it sits">

A button stays a pill or a circle inside any container; it never takes a radius matched to the surface around it. Explained in [Corners within corners](#corners-within-corners).

</Details>

<Details title="Every corner takes one of the named radii">

There are eight named radii, from none to a circle, and the tokens file says what each one is for. Shown in [The radii](#the-radii).

</Details>

<Details title="A corner reads a radius token, never a spacing token">

Radius is a shape decision, not a spacing one; a spacing value ties the corner to the spacing scale. Shown in [The radii](#the-radii).

<Details title="Nothing has a square corner">

Everything painted is rounded: a hover or selection fill, a focus ring, a skeleton, a scroll thumb, a drag handle, a bar. Nothing is too small to round: a fill a few pixels tall takes `xs`. Explained in [No sharp edges](#no-sharp-edges).

</Details>

<Details title="A stacked group rounds only its outer corners">

`none` is the radius for an edge that continues past the element, like a stacked group's inner joins or a header band across a rounded card. Shown in [No sharp edges](#no-sharp-edges).
