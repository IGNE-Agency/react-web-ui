# Elevation

Elevation is how a flat screen shows what sits on top of what. A menu that drops open, a tooltip, a message that slides in: each one floats above the page for a moment, and the eye needs to see that at a glance. Elevation keeps that clear, so the page stays put and whatever floats over it is plainly in front.

<Illustration caption="The page lies flat. Only what floats above it casts a shadow, like the one sheet lifted out of its place.">
	<ElevationDrawing />
</Illustration>

## Rising above the page

Most of a screen belongs to the page: cards, tables, forms, the bar at the top. Those all sit at one height, flat. What floats above them is what comes and goes: a menu you opened, a hint under your pointer, a message that will disappear in a few seconds. Height marks those as temporary and in front, and asks for attention without shouting.

That only works while height is rare. When everything on the page floats a little, nothing stands out, and the things that really are on top lose the one signal that sets them apart.

## The anatomy of a shadow

Light comes from above, so anything lifted off a surface blocks some of it and leaves a shadow underneath. Look closely at a real one and it has two parts. Right under the edge sits a small, dark, sharp shadow, the **umbra**, which pins the object down so it doesn't look pasted on. Around it spreads a wide, faint, blurry one, the **penumbra**, and how far that spreads is what reads as height.

Lift something higher and its penumbra grows longer and softer while the umbra stays tight. That's why a convincing shadow is never a single blur, and why a higher level gets a wider, softer one rather than just a darker one.

<Illustration caption="Lifted above the floor, a ball casts two shadows: a tight, dark core right under it and a wider, paler fringe around that.">
	<ShadowPartsDrawing />
</Illustration>

## Height in the dark

A shadow is darkness, and darkness doesn't show on something that's already dark. On a dark page a soft shadow all but disappears, so height needs a second signal: the higher a surface floats, the lighter it gets, as if it were closer to the light. That's tonal elevation. A shadow can still come along, but it's the lighter surface that does the work.

<Illustration caption="On a dark page, a surface that floats higher is a step lighter, up to the highest, which is almost white.">
	<DarkHeightDrawing />
</Illustration>

## Levels

The kit has three levels. Level 0 is the page and everything in its flow, and casts no shadow. Level 1 holds tooltips, menus, select popups and popovers. Level 2 holds toasts. Each floating level is a tight umbra plus a wide, soft penumbra, and in the dark theme each is also a lighter surface than the one below it.

## When to cast a shadow

<Beside>

A shadow earns its place only on an element that floats over the page and has no other way to say how far above it sits. Work down [the order of separation tools](?path=/docs/design-system-principles-separation--docs#work-down-space-surface-elevation-and-line-and-stop-at-the-first-that-works) first: space, then surface tier, then elevation.

A shadow is also the one separation tool that does not survive a theme. Over a dark fill a soft black penumbra is nearly invisible, which is why an overlay signals its height with [a lighter surface tier](?path=/docs/design-system-foundation-colour--docs#surfaces) and treats the shadow as reinforcement that happens to work in the light theme.

<Details title="Only an element that floats over the page casts a shadow">

Height marks what is temporary and in front: a menu, a tooltip, a popover, a toast. Everything in the page's flow sits at level 0. Explained in [Rising above the page](#rising-above-the-page).

</Details>

<Details title="A shadow is the last resort, not the first">

Work down the separation tools in order: space, then surface tier, then elevation. A shadow is for an element that floats and has no other way to say how far above the page it sits. Explained in [When to cast a shadow](#when-to-cast-a-shadow).

</Details>

<Details title="There are three levels: the page, popups and toasts">

Level 0 is the page and everything in its flow. Level 1 holds tooltips, menus, select popups and popovers. Level 2 holds toasts. Shown in [Levels](#levels).

</Details>

<Details title="A floating level is a tight umbra under a wide, soft penumbra">

A higher level gets a wider, softer penumbra, never just a darker one; the umbra stays tight at every level. Explained in [The anatomy of a shadow](#the-anatomy-of-a-shadow).

</Details>

<Details title="In the dark theme, a higher level is a lighter surface">

A shadow barely shows on a dark fill, so each floating level also reads a lighter surface role: level 1 the overlay tier, level 2 the high overlay tier. The shadow only reinforces it. Explained in [Height in the dark](#height-in-the-dark).

</Details>

<Details title="A popup with an arrow casts one shadow around the whole silhouette">

An arrow nub is a sibling element, not part of the popup box, so a `box-shadow` on the popup stops at the popup's edge and the nub reads as a detached triangle stuck to a shadowed card.

`filter: drop-shadow()` follows the composited alpha shape instead of the box. Put it on the positioner, the element wrapping both popup and arrow, and it shadows the combined outline as one object. `drop-shadow()` takes no spread, so its values are not mechanical translations of the `box-shadow` ramp and only the levels that need them are defined.

<DoDont>
<Example
  kind="do"
  caption="The positioner shadows popup and arrow together."
  visual={
    <div className={`${styles.positioner} ${styles.positionerShadowed}`}>
      <div className={styles.popup}>Last edited 12 March</div>
      <div className={styles.arrow} />
    </div>
  }>

```scss
.positioner {
	filter: var(--sys-elevation-1-filter);
}

.popup {
	@include popup.surface($shadow: false);
}
```

</Example>

```scss

}
```

</Example>
</DoDont>

<Details title="A popup with no arrow keeps box-shadow">

A popup with no arrow (tooltip, select list, typeahead, toast) keeps `box-shadow`: it is cheaper, and there is no second shape to enclose.
