# Components

A product is built from a small set of parts used over and over: a button, a field, a chip, a menu. Each one holds dozens of decisions, about its size, its colours, how it answers a pointer and what a screen reader hears, and those decisions only stay made if every job has one part. Every component listed in the sidebar already exists: extend it rather than writing a parallel version.

## One of each

Every product sooner or later grows a second button. Someone needs one that is slightly different, writing it is quicker than extending the first, and on its own screen it looks fine. That is the most expensive kind of drift, because both versions look right in isolation. Nobody notices until they disagree on a radius, a hover colour and a focus ring, and from then on every fix has to be made twice and usually isn't.

The same goes for the parts of a component nobody thinks of as components: a shape, a size, a table of tones. Written once and shared, a change lands everywhere at the same moment. Copied, it lands in one place, and the copies quietly fall behind.

So the kit has exactly one button, one text-entry treatment and one tag.

When a part from the primitive library has to be one of ours, like the close part of a dialog, it renders our component in its place instead of being restyled into a lookalike. And where it can't be our component at all, such as a foreign control that has to sit in a row and read as one of its siblings, it borrows our component's shell rather than restating it. One definition of the shape means a lookalike can't drift away from the thing it sits next to.

## Families of components

Most components belong to a family that shares a job: the fields you type in, the labels pinned to things, the navigation that frames every page, and the surfaces that float above it. A family's shared rules live on Patterns, where every member of it can find them: [fields](?path=/docs/design-system-patterns--docs#where-you-type), [chips, badges and tags](?path=/docs/design-system-patterns--docs#labels-pinned-to-things), [navigation](?path=/docs/design-system-patterns--docs#finding-your-way) and [the surfaces above the page](?path=/docs/design-system-patterns--docs#above-the-page).
